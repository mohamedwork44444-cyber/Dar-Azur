01 The Riad

A Place to Slow Down.

DAR AZUR is a boutique riad concept rooted in the quiet geometry of Moroccan architecture — shaded courtyards, carved cedar, hand-set zellige, and light that moves slowly across the walls.

Every detail is considered: the weight of a linen sheet, the temperature of the mint tea, the moment a door opens onto a courtyard instead of a street. This is hospitality measured in attention rather than volume.

Inside, contemporary restraint meets traditional craft. Outside, the blue streets of Chefchaouen rise toward the Rif mountains.

07Journal

Notes from
the house.

Short fragments of observation — imagined reflections on light, place and the craft of quiet.

Note I — On Light

The longest hour of the afternoon.

Between four and five, the courtyard stops being a room and becomes a sundial. The arches cast themselves across the tiles, and the water in the fountain moves the light one step further than you expect.

Read more

We built the riad around this hour. Rooms that face inward because inward is where the light lives best. Walls the colour of unripe apricots at noon, sand by five. It is not a light you photograph. It is a light you sit in.

Note II — On Arrival

The blue hour, in reverse.

Most guests arrive after the blue hour has already gone. The medina has been washed clean by it, and the streets are the colour of the inside of a shell. We always suggest walking out before breakfast, when the blue is at its bluest.

Read more

Chefchaouen at six in the morning belongs to brooms, cats and two or three shopkeepers rinsing the pavement. There is no reason to be out at that hour except that it is beautiful. Which is, in our opinion, a sufficient reason.

Note III — On Craft

Hands that know the work.

The zellige in the courtyard was cut by a family whose sons work in the same workshop the grandfather did. The clay, the kiln, the angle of the chisel — nothing has changed in a hundred years, which is exactly what makes it feel new.

Read more

We did not design the craft. We asked it to participate. The cedar screens were carved by hand. The plaster was troweled until it held a soft glow. We chose to spend the budget on the things you can touch.
